Where the Ocean Meets Your Soul

The World’s Most Breathtaking
Beaches

From powder-soft white sands to dramatic coastlines kissed by
turquoise waves, these beaches are more than destinations —
they’re feelings.

Tamara Walker

Author

I’ve been traveling since 2017, chasing moments instead of plans. I’m fascinated by snakes, love sharks, would happily spend weeks deep in the jungle, and can never get enough papaya. As long as there’s a next destination, I know I’m exactly where I’m meant to be.

TOP 5 beach destinations for 2026

Some beaches you visit. Others rearrange something in you and quietly refuse to give it back. I have spent the
better part of eight years chasing coastlines — some of them loud and crowded, some so still I could hear my
own heartbeat over the surf. These five are the ones I keep coming back to, and not one of them earned its
place on sand alone. It was the water, the light at six in the morning, and the particular way a good beach
stretches a single week into something that feels much longer.

Here are the five that made the 2026 list, in the order I would send you to them:

  1. Zanzibar, Tanzania. Wade in up to your knees and the reef starts right there. Sergeant majors,
    butterflyfish and the occasional Picasso triggerfish drift past close enough to touch, with nothing above you
    but coconut palms and a great deal of sky. Bring a mask and skip the organised boat tour.
  2. Palawan, Philippines. The sandbars here appear and disappear with the tide. You walk out through
    water so clear it barely registers as water, and for a few hours an entire island belongs to you and whoever
    you talked into coming along.
  3. Naxos, Greece. Golden sand instead of white, palm-thatch parasols, and a taverna fifty metres
    behind you. This is the one I recommend to anyone who wants a proper beach without a twenty-hour flight.
  4. La Digue, Seychelles. Granite boulders worn smooth over millions of years, sitting in water that
    honestly looks retouched. Rent a bicycle, because there is barely another way to get around, and stay until
    the light turns gold.
  5. The Maldives. Yes, it is on every list. It is on every list for a reason. A wooden jetty, a handful of
    moored boats, and a lagoon that works its way through four different blues before lunch.
Pro tipps
  1. Go early, or go late. Between 11:00 and 15:00 the light is flat, the sand gets hot enough to hurt and every beach on this list is at its busiest. Sunrise costs you one alarm and hands you the whole place. Useful link. Check the tide table before you plan a sandbar day — on Palawan it decides whether there is an island at all.
  2. Reef-safe sunscreen is not marketing. Oxybenzone and octinoxate bleach coral, and several of these destinations
    have banned them outright. Buy a mineral-based one before you fly: on a small island it will cost you three times
    as much, assuming you find it at all.
If you take one thing from this list, make it this — the beach is rarely the hard part, getting the season right is. Zanzibar and the Maldives are at their best from December through April, when the rain holds off and the water settles into that impossible flat calm. Palawan runs on much the same rhythm. The Seychelles are more forgiving; there is no genuinely bad month, only windier ones. Greece belongs to late May, June and September, before and after the crowds arrive. Book the flight around the weather rather than the other way round, and everything else on a good beach tends to sort itself out.
